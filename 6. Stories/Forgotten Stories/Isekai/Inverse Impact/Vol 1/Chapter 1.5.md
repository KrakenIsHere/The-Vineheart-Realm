# Chapter 1.5: The Gentle Calamity

Now that things had gone quiet, I could hear water running along the lower passage. I took that branch and followed the gutter along the wall, still turning at small sounds, until its water gathered in a shallow basin. My first handful scarcely reached my mouth before I wanted another. I drank, then sat with my shoulder beside the basin and let my feet stop carrying me for a while. Behind me were the failing lift, the wolf, and a series of freshly opened holes that seemed more useful to monsters than to rescuers.

The water tasted of stone and the metal basin. I drank more slowly after the first handful, then wet a corner of my shirt and wiped the dust from my lip. The cut on my forearm had dried into a stiff line beneath the sleeve. I rinsed around it and tied a strip of cloth over it, awkwardly, with my teeth helping more than my right hand. The window still listed me as injured, and the ache under my ribs caught me whenever I shifted. I settled back more carefully and drew the cloak around my shoulders against the draft, grateful for the ring's faint warmth in my cold hand.

I stretched out my legs and checked my pockets, finding my wallet and the phone I was afraid to turn on. Ordan's old gloves were still tucked inside my jacket, and the sword at my hip had survived the wolf with two fresh tooth marks in its blade. With the cloak, ring, and new gloves, I had more useful things than before, but nothing to eat. My stomach had been reminding me of that for some time; water had only given it something else to complain about.

Through the narrow doorway beside the basin, I could see shelves crowded with broken baskets and several lidded pottery jars. I drew my feet beneath me and got up at the thought of bread, too quickly for my bruised hip. After waiting for the ache to ease, I shuffled through the doorway and approached the shelves. I would have accepted bread old enough to require a formal apology.

I lifted the first lid, found an empty jar, and set the lid aside before moving along the shelf. A pale growth covered the clumped grains in the next jar, so I lowered its lid again and tried the third. That one had cracked, spilling dark seeds across the shelf, some partly eaten. I picked up a few and carried them beneath the lamp, hoping whatever small creature had found meals here knew something I did not. The seeds were shriveled, hard, and unfamiliar. I lifted one toward my mouth, then hesitated; I could not tell whether I was looking at spoiled food, perfectly normal food, or something people here fed exclusively to animals. I took them back to the shelf and put them down, keeping my face clear of whatever was growing beneath the lower shelf. The translation spell had not provided grocery training.

While I was checking the last basket, a mouse emerged from a crack beside the door, and we both stopped: it with its nose lifted, me with a basket lid in my hand. It was small, brown, and entirely without armor, and no window appeared beside it. Its only apparent qualification for living here was that it could fit through places I could not. "Hello," I said quietly, absurdly glad of the company.

Its ears moved. It stayed beside the crack.

"I am having a difficult day." That seemed to be enough information. The mouse slipped beneath the shelf, found one of the fallen seeds, and carried it back toward the doorway. I lowered the basket lid and crouched, keeping a comfortable distance between my hands and the floor.

After pausing to sniff a loose thread beside my wrapped foot, the mouse went to the basin. I watched it drink, its nose twitching between mouthfuls, until it stopped to wash its face without once looking up at me. I found myself breathing more quietly so I would not frighten it. The room held only the trickle and those small movements. I still glanced at the doorway whenever something clicked in the gutter. It took effort to believe I could stay here without waiting for the next attack.

I picked up a seed from the shelf and placed it beside the wall, reminding myself that carrying things and setting them down had been safe all morning. The mouse waited until my hand was well away before collecting it. "You are welcome," I said.

It sat on its haunches to eat, turning the seed between its front paws. I smiled before I noticed that my hand had lifted from my knee. The fur between its ears looked soft. Then I remembered the warmth of the wolf beneath my palm and folded my hands in my lap, keeping them there. The mouse had come close enough to share the room. That could be enough.

I stayed until it finished eating and disappeared through a hole beside the gutter, with the ease of a creature that had already solved the problem of living here. The opening was narrower than my wrist. I was glad the mouse had somewhere to go. Back beside the basin, I checked the knot over my forearm while a small ordinary creature ate breakfast somewhere behind the wall. It was doing that at the same time as whatever the monsters were doing; the place contained more than things waiting for me to enter sword range.

I thought of my mother calling to ask whether I was eating. Usually I told her yes while deciding what to buy on the way home. She would have questions when I failed to answer. So would my manager, though his would arrive with considerably less concern about my lunch. With no signal, there was little reason to switch on the phone and watch the nearly empty battery in the corner of its screen. I kept it off, saving what remained for a photograph of a useful sign or a little light when the lamps stopped working.

*1. Find food. 2. Find a way out. 3. Stay alive long enough to need both.* The order might require adjustment. At least I had a list.

As I rose, I watched the water arriving from somewhere above and thought of the people who had built and maintained the channel. They had used the room beside it for stores and carried loads between levels on the stairs and platforms, coming here for reasons that did not involve fighting. I wanted to find one of those routes before I damaged it.

The gloves gave me a little more room for mistakes: when a floor tile tipped beneath my foot and I caught myself against an iron bracket, the silver stitches warmed and the bracket bent a little instead of tearing out of the wall. A deliberate push on a loose stone still sent it shooting across the room. I was dangerous, even with help.

I took the sword out to practice where the corridor widened. A hard swing barely marked a rotten post. Moving the blade gently cut through it and scored a deep groove in the stone behind it. A pebble thrown normally bounced along the floor; one tossed with almost no effort buried itself in a distant slab. I had seen versions of both experiments in the palace. Repeating them alone was less educational than I had hoped. Nobody could tell me whether I was doing it wrong, or doing it right, which was what frightened me.

What worried me most was how difficult it was to produce the same result twice. One tap that seemed gentle broke a tile, while another broke a whole step, though afterward I found an old crack running through its underside. The stone mattered, as did the difference between what I meant to do and what my cold, shaking fingers actually did. I had no useful way to measure either before something broke. *Would a handshake destroy someone? Would a high-five remove the arm? Could I ever pet a cat again?*

I stopped with the sword half raised. "No. That is unacceptable." It came out with more anger than I expected. Getting home, finding food, and avoiding another sword mattered; I would still like to have something waiting on the other side of survival besides a lifetime of apologizing from across the room. I put the sword away and closed my hands carefully. I would learn to control this cursed skill, escape the dungeon, and find the people who had summoned me so I could make them finish the job properly. And someday, I would pet a cat. Normally.

I left the splintered post and walked along the corridor again. The gutter continued beside the wall. I followed it beneath two more blue lamps, keeping clear of the loose stones I had used for practice. The floor sloped down toward a bend that hid the passage ahead.

A deep rumble reached me through the soles of my feet, making me stop before the bend as little ripples crossed the gutter. Metal scraped against stone somewhere ahead. I brought my hand toward the sword and waited for a step that would tell me something was coming around the corner, but none followed; even then, I did not move until the water settled. *Please let that be machinery.*

Once the sound faded, I crept along the wall and around the bend. The corridor ended at a doorway high enough to admit a wagon, blue light spilling across its threshold. I stopped short of it to listen again. From there, I could see a chamber several times wider than the passage, its floor covered with bones and broken weapons and its walls lined with empty nooks beneath blue lamps. Near the center, a stone counterweight hung in an iron cradle, its chains rising into a square shaft in the ceiling. Rust flaked from the supporting rails, and one long brace joined the cradle to a cracked pillar just inside the doorway, on my left.

Under it sat something the size of a small house. Black scales covered its shoulders. Four arms rested on the floor around it, each beside a weapon too large for a person to lift. Two horns curved backward from its head. An old sword stood through its chest, the hilt rising between its collarbones. That should have been encouraging evidence that someone else had already dealt with the problem.

I stayed at the threshold, trying to see past the heap beneath the counterweight. The far wall was lost behind it. One step would give me a better angle, and the doorway would still be at my back. My shoe touched the chamber floor.

Its golden eyes opened.

I stopped just inside the doorway, afraid even of shifting my feet. For a moment, neither of us moved. Then its gaze settled on me. A red window replaced the blue one.

> **Grave Tyrant Malgorth**
>
> **Creature:** Gravebound Colossus (assembled zombie).
> **Creature Type:** Undead.
> **Size:** Huge.
> **Hit Points:** 168 / 168.
> **Armor Class:** 18 (natural armor).
> **Condition:** Active.
> **Traits:** Multiattack.
>
> **Threat:** Lethal.
> **Recommendation:** RUN.

"Oh. Oh, n—" The rest caught in my throat as Malgorth rose in a rattle of bones sliding from its lap. Its upper hands closed around a battle-axe and a broad sword, while the lower two collected a hooked blade and something that looked like an iron door attached to a handle.

"Oh no!" Its first step shifted bones across the floor. The old sword moved with its chest, apparently more of an accessory than a wound. I backed toward the threshold, keeping my eyes on the nearest weapon.

A chain rattled above my head. I turned just as a stone slab dropped across the entrance, close enough for its rush of air to touch my face. It struck the floor with a crash, and the corridor vanished. I stood staring at the stone where the way out had been. The lamps brightened as though somebody wanted an excellent view of what happened next. "Of course!"

I put my palm against the slab. The stone was cold and solid, and I wanted to push before I had even looked up at the chains. I made myself stop. If the doorway came down with it, I would have to get through falling stone.

A shadow crossed my hand.

I turned and stumbled sideways as the axe struck where my feet had been. Bones rolled beneath my shoe, dropping me onto my back. The broad sword passed above my face; I felt its air move my hair before it struck somewhere beyond me. Pieces of stone hit my back as I rolled onto my side and crawled for the cracked pillar. The hooked blade dragged through the heap behind my legs. I pulled them in and squeezed into the space behind the pillar, beneath the brace joining it to the cradle.

A sting in my calf drew my attention to a tear in my pants, where blood was running into my remaining shoe. I could still put weight on the leg, which was the full medical examination I had time for. Malgorth pulled the axe free and turned toward the pillar.

My sword's grip was slick inside my glove when I drew it. All I had to do was move it gently enough to make contact, just as I had practiced. Unfortunately, my shoulders were locked, my teeth were clenched, and every part of me wanted to hit the thing as hard as possible. *Hard swings are useless. Fear makes me tense. Tensing makes me push harder.*

I looked at the four weapons gathering above the pillar. *So the more terrified I become, the worse I fight. Excellent design.* "You have got to be kidding me."

The hook scraped around the pillar. I tried to pull the cloak in after my knee, but the blade caught its edge and the collar snapped tight against my throat. My next breath stopped halfway in. I bent backward, shoulder scraping stone, and got my fingers beneath the collar. A firm pull gave me enough room to breathe, though the fabric stayed taut. Every movement of the hook tugged me toward the pillar's edge.

I could let the cloak go. That would leave me with cold hands, a bleeding leg, and three useful items of which one had recently become a leash. I hated how long it took to make a decision that obvious.

The hook grated against the pillar. Its shaft was wood, bound with dark metal near the blade. I had cut a post with this sword a few minutes earlier. I looked at the shaft, then at my blade, and tried to remember the movement instead of the monster holding the other end. I lowered the sword toward the shaft, stopping when the edge settled against wood. My right hand wanted to squeeze harder. I loosened my shoulder instead, keeping the blade in place while the collar pressed against my throat.

A small, deliberate push split the haft. The hooked end dropped beside my knee, and the cloak fell loose across my chest. I drew a breath that hurt, pulled the fabric free, and scrambled farther behind the pillar before the monster could bring another weapon around it. *That worked. I actually meant to do that.* For a moment, the relief was enormous. I had chosen a target, reached it, and produced something useful. There had been no accidental hole in a wall and no need to explain myself to anyone afterward.

Malgorth looked at the short piece of wood in its lower hand and let it fall. Three weapons remained.

The broad sword scraped over the top of the pillar. I crouched until my nose almost touched my knee. Its edge knocked stone loose above me, and the pieces landed on my shoulders. Malgorth gave me no time to finish congratulating myself. An axe strike stopped me from getting around the other side, scattering fragments beneath my shoe. I almost fell with my weight half shifted. My calf had begun to throb, hurting when I stood on it and more when I tried to move quickly.

The entrance slab was only a few paces away. If I could get a hand against it and choose where to push, I might make an opening. I looked at the doorway while Malgorth worked the axe out of the floor. Above the slab, a chain ran to a wheel fixed beneath the ceiling. A second length crossed toward the counterweight's frame. I could not tell what would come down if I broke the stone holding them. The platform upstairs had looked usable until a piece fell out of it. This mechanism was attached to something that could put an axe through the floor. I would have to reach the slab before I could make any of those questions matter.

I put one foot toward the entrance while Malgorth worked the axe free. Its lower arm moved at once. The iron door swept through the space in front of me, close enough that I could see the raised rust around its dents. I pulled back behind the pillar, scraping my injured calf against a broken step. The pain made me hold the leg still for a moment. The doorway was only a few paces away, but I could not find a way to take the first one.

*I cannot run around four arms. I can barely run around one piece of furniture.* I had been thinking of the Skill as a way to make any weapon useful. It did nothing for the time it took me to get my feet underneath me. I needed cover, a reachable target, and a moment when the monster's weapons were somewhere else. So far, I had been getting those moments by falling down.

Malgorth set its feet wider. I watched the upper sword rise, then saw its lower hand turn the iron door toward the pillar. There was nowhere farther behind the stone to go. I held my sword across my body and tried to make myself small.

The iron door came down. The pillar cracked beside my ear, and the brace sagged with a metallic groan. I lurched away as stone scraped my shoulder. My sword caught between two fallen pieces, jerking my hand to a stop. I pulled once. The hilt moved; the blade did not. Malgorth stepped over the wreckage, lifting its axe. I could keep pulling or get my hand free.

I let go of the sword. My empty hand was shaking so badly that I could see the silver stitches move. *Relax. Relax. Relax.* A monster was about to turn me into paste. There was no reason for my body to find this instruction persuasive.

I shut my eyes for the length of a breath. At first, all I could hear was the metal shifting above me. I tried to put another sound beside it: rain against my apartment window, the quiet click of a cup set down after tea. The old blanket was over my knees, its thin corner tucked beneath my foot. The alarm was still several hours away. I had to let the breath out before any of that felt real enough to help. My shoulders loosened enough to let me uncurl my fingers, though bravery remained well out of reach.

The axe struck the rubble beside me. I tried to scramble backward, but a broken spear caught my wrapped foot and turned me sideways. When I looked up, Malgorth was bent over the fallen pillar, close enough that I could see grit lodged between its black scales. Its nearest arm stretched over the broken pillar, leaving its stomach within reach. I ducked beneath it because moving away had stopped being possible, and brought my left hand toward its scales. My fingers were open. I concentrated on keeping them that way.

I rested my palm against its scales with the care of someone trying to comfort a crying child. "There, there."

Malgorth looked down at me. *Please be enough.*

I gave the smallest push I could manage, away from me.

Malgorth staggered backward three heavy steps, its weapons falling from its hands, and struck the hanging counterweight. The damaged brace snapped. Rusted rails tore out of cracked sockets, the cradle tipped, and the counterweight fell across the monster. Chains dragged through the overhead pulleys with a crash that seemed to go on far longer than one crash had any right to.

I threw myself beneath the pillar's remains as the chains screamed overhead. Through a gap between two stones, I saw a pulley mounting wrench free of the shaft wall. The masonry broke around it, and pieces of the cover struck the floor close enough to make me curl my knees toward my chest.

Rubble kept coming. I held my forearm over my head and looked through the gap whenever the dust thinned. Beyond the loose chains and the broken cover, a pale patch appeared.

Actual daylight.

For one beautiful second, I forgot everything except the fact that the sky still existed. Then a chunk of stone the size of my head landed beside my knee.

"Oh, come on!" With dust filling my mouth, I crawled farther beneath the broken pillar as the iron cradle settled with a crash nearby. Loose stones lodged across the shaft, narrowing the daylight to a gap too high to reach and too small to offer any immediate escape.

When the falling stopped, one horn and part of a scaled hand showed beneath the counterweight. Neither moved. The red window faded, and two blue ones crowded into its place.

> **Grave Tyrant Malgorth defeated.**
>
> **Hit Points:** 0 / 168.
> **Condition:** Dead.
>
> **Impact recorded:** Very low applied force.
> **Designation updated:** Gentle Calamity.
>
> Additional curse assessment available.

> **Name:** Haruto Minase.
>
> **Level:** 2.
> **Class:** None.
> **Hit Points:** 2 / 7.
> **Armor Class:** 12 (unarmored).
> **Spell Slots:** None.
>
> **Condition:** Injured, Fatigued.
> **Occupation:** None.
> **Designation:** Gentle Calamity.
> **Title:** Gentle Hands.

I read my new designation twice, too exhausted to find it funny. "Gentle Calamity?"

I looked at the buried monster, the broken cradle, and the shaft I had briefly mistaken for a rescue plan. My calf was bleeding. My sword was trapped somewhere under the debris. The new designation had left my level and armor exactly where they were, and my hit points had fallen to two. I had won the fight by being gentle, and all the damage was now my problem. "Could we concentrate on the calamity part? I think that is the useful information."

Another window appeared.

> Reveal additional conditions?
>
> **YES / NO**

I crawled out from under the stones and sat against the wall. My stomach cramped with hunger. The new gloves were covered in gray dust, and my left hand was still shaking. I wanted an explanation. I wanted a weakness I could work around, a rule I could actually measure, or a sentence that began with 'To remove this curse.'

Before answering, I pulled the torn fabric clear of my calf. Blood had made it stick, and lifting it brought back the sting I had barely noticed during the fight. I folded the loose flap into a pad and held it over the cut, then took a strip from the cloth around my other ankle to keep it in place. The knot would not come together, and I watched my stiff right fingers miss the same loop twice before stopping when they cramped. After a breath, I tried again and managed to make it hold. The bleeding slowed, though the pain remained, and I leaned my head against the wall to listen to the machinery settle.

Through the gap in the shaft, the daylight had a warmer color than the lamps. I could see a little cloud moving past it. Somewhere above me, wind was crossing a mountain. People were walking along roads, eating meals, and doing things that would not fit under **Catastrophic Pacifist**. "Help," I called, desperate for someone to answer.

My voice traveled up the shaft. I kept my head lifted after it, waiting so hard that every little shift of stone sounded almost like an answer. Nothing came. I tried again, louder, and the dust in my throat made me cough. A few grains fell from the broken cover. I watched them land on my knees.

The opening was high above the counterweight, with nothing I could climb safely between them. Even if I reached it, I would still have to get past the stones lodged across the shaft. For now, it was a view of somewhere I wanted to be.

Part of my sword's hilt showed beneath chunks of stone piled against the broken cradle, but I left it where it was. Pulling that arrangement apart could wait until I had stopped shaking.

The cloak had a small tear where the hook caught it. I brought its edges together over my knees and felt the ring's warmth return as the sweat cooled on my hand. I had survived. It seemed reasonable to spend a moment understanding that before I tried to do anything else. Then I looked back at the waiting question. For once, the window was offering information instead of documenting my humiliation after the event. *An explanation. That would be new.*

I stared at YES. The letters brightened under my gaze. "How much worse could this possibly get?" The question came out hoarse. I swallowed the dust in my throat and made myself answer. "Yes."

> **Additional condition:** Affection Multiplier.

"Affection?" More writing appeared beneath the heading.

> **Affection Multiplier**
>
> The more sincere the bearer's emotional attachment to the target, the greater the resulting impact.
>
> - Applies when Inverse Impact activates.
> - Ordinary contact remains safe.
> - Maximum impact unchanged.

I sat very still. I glanced at Malgorth. I had not become emotionally attached to it. "There, there" had been a desperate attempt to keep my shoulders loose. Apparently the window had saved this detail until after I had formed plans involving an animal I actually wanted to touch.

I thought of the mouse beside the basin and the impulse to rub a finger between its ears. I had been worried about shaking hands. I had been worried about using a sword. Before entering this room, I had made what seemed a reasonable decision: surviving long enough to pet a cat was a goal worth pursuing. *A cat I actually like would be worse.* I lowered my hand into my lap. "No," I said miserably.

A final line appeared beneath the explanation.

> **Warning: Kissing is not recommended.**

For a moment, I could hear nothing except my own breathing. Then I shouted, "NOOOOOOOOOOOOO!"

The word rose through the shaft and came back at me from the upper passages as something below the chamber shifted with a slow, heavy scrape. A chain hanging through a crack in the floor drew taut, and I watched one link fall, then another. Whatever was pulling it was much farther down than I wanted anything to be.

A low sound from beneath the floor stopped my shouting, growing until I could feel it through the wall at my back. *Please be machinery.*

Something roared. It sounded awake.
