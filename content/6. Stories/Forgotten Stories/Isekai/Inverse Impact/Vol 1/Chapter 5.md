"It broke the pillar first," I said.

Nessa stood beside me on the landing, with the open gate behind us. Through the narrow windows on our left, I could see the reservoir. The wooden pipe ran along the wall on our right. Ahead, the stairs climbed around the corner where her lantern light had first appeared.

Her eyes moved from my face to the empty scabbard. "Start before the pillar."

I started again.

There was a limit to how much dignity I could preserve while describing a fight I had spent mostly on the floor. I explained the closing door, the four arms, and the weapons. I told her how the hooked blade had caught my cloak, how the pillar had broken, and how I had lost the sword beneath it. When I reached the part where I had put my hand against the monster's stomach, she lowered the lantern a little.

"You got that close?"

"It got that close to me."

"And you pushed it into the weight."

"Yes. The frame was already damaged. The weight came down, then something broke higher up the shaft."

While she considered that, water moved inside the pipe beside us and a chain rattled intermittently beneath the windows.

"That fits," she said at last.

I would have preferred an explanation that did not.

"I think I started the collapse."

Nessa looked at me instead of the pipe.

"You said the pillar broke before you touched the guard."

"It did. But I sent it into the machinery."

I nearly added that it had been trying to kill me. She had heard that part already. Beside us, the chain rattled again, and water knocked against the wooden planks.

I kept my hands at my sides.

"I needed to get away," I said. "I did not know you were down here."

"You know now."

"Yes."

She watched me a moment longer, then lifted the lantern toward the stairs. I moved aside to let her pass, wondering whether she would still want me to follow.

"Then we work with what is still standing."

I went after her before she could get beyond the light.

I followed her up the first flight. At the turn, she stopped beside a low arch leading left into a small room. The stairs continued upward on our right, toward a door with a white stripe painted across it.

"The service stair," she said, pointing to that door. "You can look from the threshold."

The door dragged as she opened it, its hinges complaining in much the same voice as every other hinge I had met. Beyond a short passage, the next flight of stairs continued on the far side of a broken landing, separated from us by a gap wider than I was tall. Pale stone and torn iron lined both edges, with water catching the lantern light far below.

I stayed beside the door while Nessa held the lantern farther out. Three dry steps came into view on the other side, with more continuing upward beyond the light.

If I had reached this landing yesterday, I could have walked across it.

I put my weight back onto the foot nearest the passage. Pale fragments crowded the edge in front of us, and far below, the water caught another small glint from the lantern.

*An exit can be real and still be completely useless to me.*

"Do people know you are here?" I asked.

"The crew knows which pipe I came to repair."

"Will they come looking?"

"They should. Whether they can reach this side is another question."

She pulled the door shut. I heard the latch settle before she turned back toward the low arch.

"The other steps join that flight higher up. We need the inspection ledge to reach them."

After one more glance at the white stripe, I followed her through the low arch into the room.

It was a control room, though considerably smaller than the words made me expect. A narrow window overlooked this end of the reservoir. Below it, a little work platform projected beside the outlet weight's iron frame. Five stone steps descended from our room to the platform. A closed hatch at the platform's far corner covered another stair going down toward the water.

Once Nessa hung the lantern inside, I went to the window to watch the stone weight moving between two upright rails. An iron sheet covered the side facing the work platform, its lower edge folded inward and scraped bright where the moving cradle caught it.

The weight descended toward the fold. I leaned forward to see where the two would meet.

Iron struck iron with a bang that drove me back from the window. The cradle shuddered, the chain pulled tight, and the weight began rising again.

I put my hand against my thigh rather than the sill. From this close, I could feel each impact through the soles of my feet.

"That is the outlet?"

"The weight opens it. A chain goes over the pulley and down to the gate in the channel. When the weight comes down, the gate goes up."

I followed her pointing finger from the cradle to the chain above it, then across the pulley toward a narrow shaft beside the reservoir wall.

"It cannot come down far enough," she said. "The gate barely opens. Water pushes it shut again, and the weight comes back up."

As the weight repeated its journey, I noticed that the water had passed a dark line on the wall. Another stain higher up remained dry.

"And it has been doing that since yesterday?"

"With a few pauses when I thought I had persuaded it to improve."

A wrench and two wooden wedges rested well clear of the moving cradle on the platform below. One wedge had a crushed end. Beside the hatch was a hammer, placed where someone could reach it without leaning into the frame.

Nessa had been down there with those tools while I ate her food and slept under the table.

I looked at her, then back at the platform. There was room to stand behind the guard. There was also room to fall, and my calf still ached from the stairs.

"What do we need to do?"

"Stop the weight above the damage. Remove that bent cover. Then let it descend far enough to lift the gate."

"Can I help?"

She glanced at my hands.

"You can carry things."

I glanced at the crushed wedge. I had asked because I could break almost anything she pointed to, and she needed something unbroken to hold that weight.

"All right," I said, before she had to explain it.

I moved away from the window to give her room.

A cupboard stood beside a stool and a board of unreadable markings, with a coil of thick rope hanging alongside it. From its bottom shelf, Nessa took a cloth bag and shook it open, revealing rust stains around a hole near the seam.

"First, I want something to eat," she said.

"The workroom?"

"You made a route to it. Show me."

She took the lantern from its hook and lifted the empty bag.

---

At the service gate below the stairs, I stopped beside the iron bar still resting where Nessa had asked me to leave it.

"May I take that?"

Nessa looked at it, then at me.

"Carry it with the wedge pointed down."

As I picked it up by the middle, she closed the gate behind us, using the wrench to lift it enough for the latch to settle. The key stayed in the lock on our side.

"So we can get back in," she said.

I had been about to ask.

We followed the pipe out through the covered passage and down the four steps. The reservoir was on our right now. I kept the wall on my left. At the opening beneath the arch, I pointed out the bent rail and the empty socket where its fastening had been.

Nessa stopped well short of it.

"You pushed the stone from that side?"

"Toward the water."

A few pieces of mortar came away beneath her fingers as she crouched to examine the opening. Watching her brush them aside, I found myself jealous of someone cleaning a doorway.

"The arch is holding," she said. "Keep your head low."

She went through first with the lantern close to the floor, and I followed with the bar held clear of the loose pieces. When my pad caught on a stone, I stopped to free it before continuing. There was no prize for doing this quickly.

On the other side, I showed her the charcoal arrow beside the steps. From there, I led her through the second round room, back over the little channel bridge, and through the racks. At the first round room, my three pebbles still marked the passage toward the sorting-room landing.

Nessa paused beneath the signpost.

"Do you want to know what these say?"

"Very much."

The boards named the sorting gallery, an upper channel, and a drain passage. She pointed to each in turn.

I copied short Japanese labels onto the slate with firm charcoal strokes. Then I added a little arrow beside the route we had just used. None of the boards said anything about leaving.

"Does this place have an exit sign?"

"Several."

"I would enjoy meeting one."

At the broken crossing, she held the lantern toward the fallen iron. She examined the pale sockets, the torn supports, and the carts beneath them. Her mouth tightened.

"I crossed that yesterday."

She held the lantern steady over the gap. I followed its light down to the carts, trying to picture her above them, carrying the bundle I had found.

The hanging chain tapped a cart.

I had been pleased to find daylight after the collapse. Nessa had come back to a crossing that wasn't there. When she lowered the lantern, I stepped out of her way and let her choose the next passage.

We left the broken crossing behind and passed through the sorting room, where the skeleton still dragged its scraper along the trough. Nessa followed the aisle to the door at the stairs without looking toward it.

I kept pace with her, watching the wooden handle rise and fall beyond the rail.

"Does it ever stop?" I whispered.

"Not while I have been here."

"Does anyone ask it to?"

"I repair the water gates."

That seemed to be the limit of the services available.

At the bottom of the stairs, I recognized my triangle of pebbles. We followed the gutter to the basin, and I stopped across from the open workroom.

The workroom was as I had left it, with the wooden bar beside the door, the bench out of its way, and my coin on the table beside the food. Nessa went straight to the bundle. I had never been so relieved to watch someone untie a piece of cloth.

She set the lantern on the table, took bread and cheese, filled the flask at the basin, and came back to sit on the bench. I remained near the table, holding the bar until she pointed to an empty patch of floor.

"You can put that down."

I did.

She cut the cheese with a little knife from her apron and passed me a piece of bread with some on top. I looked at the food, then at the coin.

"That was my last silver."

I kept the bread in my hand while she glanced at the coin.

"I will account for it when we get back."

"Does it cover the pads?"

"The pads are borrowed. So is the tool."

"I would like to return them."

"That is usually how borrowing works."

She went back to cutting her own piece of cheese. I waited another moment, then took a bite. She had given me food without asking what my hands could do in return.

At the other end of the bench, I ate cheese that was hard, salty, and considerably more welcome than its appearance suggested. Across the corridor, the basin splashed, stopped, and began trickling again, making Nessa pause to listen as she chewed.

"Has it been doing that all night?"

"Yes."

"Then the outlet is still catching."

She looked tired enough that I wanted to suggest sleeping under the table. The water answered that idea with another spurt over the rim.

"Should we hurry?"

"We should eat. Then we should get the weight secured before we put anything near it."

I finished the piece she had given me. When she offered another, I accepted without discussing the finances again.

For a few minutes, I could sit with both feet on the floor and eat with the person who had brought the food. Nessa chewed beside me, occasionally setting down her bread to hear the basin.

I stopped trying to finish before she changed her mind and let myself taste the cheese.

*A chair would improve this considerably. I am willing to start with bread.*

Nessa pocketed the coin and packed the remaining food, the flask, and a tin of grease. Several wooden wedges followed from the shelf, each checked for splits before she chose it.

I unwrapped the slate from my cloak.

"Could this go in there too?"

She held the bag open for my slate, the old gloves containing cord and charcoal, and the spare pad. With my belt relieved of several responsibilities, I put the cloak back around my shoulders.

Nessa glanced at its silver stitching, then at the gloves I was wearing.

"Where did those come from?"

"A chest fell out of a shelf after I broke three skeletons."

She waited.

I considered whether there was a version of that sentence worth trying again.

"The gloves help when I lose my balance," I said. "A little. They do not stop me breaking things on purpose."

"Good to know."

"They are good gloves."

"I believe you."

It occurred to me that I was defending the most reassuring gift I had found in a room full of body parts. Nessa tied the bag closed before I could make that sound any better.

At the doorway, she stopped.

"Where were you trying to go before you fell?"

"A survey lodge. On the southern road. Past a small bridge and three white stakes."

"The survey road is above the old workings. You came down through the forest?"

"Through the forest, then through the floor."

"Did someone send you to the lodge?"

"The palace."

Her look reminded me that the man who had robbed me had advised against mentioning that part. Keeping it secret now would require lying to someone who had shared her bread and knew the stairs.

"I was summoned," I said. "Apparently they wanted a champion."

"You?"

I looked down at my repaired foot, then at the little bar on the floor.

"There were some problems with the result."

Nessa rested the bag against her hip.

"How long ago?"

"A few days. I came from somewhere very far away."

I did not know how to explain a train to someone standing beside a brazier. I could explain losing a shoe. We had started with something that translated well.

"They could not keep me near the terraces after the tests," I said. "They told me to stay at the lodge while they looked for a way to send me home."

"They sent you alone?"

"Ordan took me to the watchpost. Then something needed repairing, and he went back. I was supposed to follow the main road."

"What happened?"

I told her about the men, the bag, and the slimes. I kept it short. There were only so many occasions on which I could explain that I had lost everything without beginning to sound like a person who misplaced things professionally.

Nessa looked toward the corridor when I finished.

"Did the keeper ever see you?"

"I never reached the lodge."

"Then whoever goes there to look for you will find an empty room."

I stopped folding the edge of my cloak. I had been waiting for the keeper to find me, or for Ordan to come down the road. Neither of them knew which way I had run after the men took my bag.

If they went to the lodge, there would be nothing to tell them I had almost reached it.

"The princess wanted someone to come after me," I said. "They did not promise when."

Even that small hope depended on someone looking in the wrong place.

Nessa adjusted the bag's strap over her shoulder.

"We get out first. Then you can tell someone which room to look in."

It was the first plan involving the palace that began with something I could understand.

When Nessa took the lantern from the table, I picked up the bar to follow her.

---

When we reached the control room again, the water had climbed closer to the upper stain on the wall. The weight still struck the bent cover on every descent. Nessa put the bag on the cupboard and took out the wedges.

"Stand here," she said, placing the lantern on the windowsill. "Tell me when the cradle comes back up."

I stayed at the window while she went down the five steps to the work platform. The weight moved below and a little to my right; Nessa stood on its left, outside the rails and behind a waist-high iron guard.

Lifting a long handle beside the frame brought a catch toward the teeth of a wheel above her, while the chain rattled through another short movement.

"Coming up," I said.

"Keep watching."

The cradle rose above the folded edge. I watched the gap widen, afraid that if I spoke too soon she would stop it against the iron.

"Above it."

Nessa pulled the handle down. The catch met the toothed wheel with a clack, and the chain jerked tight.

I waited for the weight to descend again.

It stayed where it was. Beneath it, the clear space remained open, and water rushed through the shaft without the bang I had begun expecting after every few breaths.

After checking the chain and catch, Nessa tried to fit a thick pin through a second hole beside the handle. When it stopped short, she withdrew it and moved the handle a fraction. The pin went through on her second attempt.

"Does that hold it?"

"The catch holds the wheel. The pin keeps the handle from moving."

I nodded once her pointing finger had helped me tell the two apart.

"Come down. Stay on the steps until I tell you where to stand."

With the lantern from the sill, I followed her onto a platform with room for two beside the guard, provided neither decided to be somewhere else suddenly. The hatch was behind Nessa near the wall, and below the guard the cover's lower edge bent into the cradle's path.

She placed the wedges beneath a ledge on the cradle, using the head of her wrench to slide them into the gap from the side. Her hands stayed outside the frame.

"If the catch slips, those should stop it before the bend," she said. "We still keep out from underneath."

I looked up at the chain. Several links were polished where they crossed the pulley. Beyond that, rust darkened them again.

I had watched a chain tear stone out of a ceiling. It had given me enough information about the arrangement of my body beneath heavy objects.

Nessa crouched beside the damaged cover. Its bottom edge had caught against the side of the cradle. Four bolts fixed it to stone sockets outside the rails. The cover did not carry the weight. It prevented people from putting hands into the guide while it moved.

The purpose seemed clear enough. I regretted having a reason to learn it.

Nessa fitted the wrench to the lowest bolt and took up the slack. Her shoulders tightened. For a moment, I thought the bolt was turning.

The wrench slipped and struck the platform.

I drew back, lifting the lantern out of the way. She stayed crouched, looking at her hand before wiping a line of rust from her knuckles. Then she fitted the wrench from a different angle and tried again.

This time, I watched the socket instead of the tool. Nothing moved.

"That one was already bad," she said, releasing the pressure. "The shift has pulled it sideways."

I held the lantern closer for her to examine the socket, then watched her try the bolt above it. That one came free slowly, with a rough scrape at each turn. After setting it in a hollow beside the hatch, she removed the other upper bolt and worked the cover's top edge loose.

The sheet shifted toward her around its two jammed lower bolts.

"Could we leave it like that?" I asked.

"It would catch the cradle again."

My bar's wedge was narrow enough to fit the gap she had opened.

"Could I try this? With firm pressure."

She studied the bar, then pointed to a space at the top of the fold.

"Here. Away from the rail. Keep the end against the cover."

I set down the lantern and fitted the wedge into the gap. Nessa remained beside the steps, where she could see both my hand and the catch above the weight.

I pushed firmly, watching the folded edge. It moved away from the cradle with a creak, and I held the pressure, hoping the lower part would follow.

The bar started sliding sideways.

I stopped before its wedge came free. The lower bolts hadn't moved. Setting my feet wider, I fitted the end back into the gap and tried again. My right calf tightened; the iron bent beneath the wedge, slowly taking a small dent.

Still nothing came loose.

There was no hidden supply of ordinary strength beneath my curse. I had become very powerful at the part of the job involving making the wrong amount of effort.

"Enough," Nessa said. "You will slip."

I was about to tell her I could try once more. Then I looked down at my left foot, with its leather pad turned slightly outward, and lifted the bar clear.

The lowest stair gave me somewhere to stand without needing the iron to yield.

Although the weight was still, the water kept rising.

"We can chisel the heads off," she said, looking at the two bolts still holding the fold. "It will take time."

I could break timber. I could break stone. I had also broken enough things around both to understand why she was reaching for the chisel.

"I might be able to break that part," I said.

Nessa considered that before answering. It was an improvement over being asked to do it before anyone considered the room.

"How much would break?" she asked.

"I do not know. I can choose where I push. I cannot promise how far something will go."

She put the chisel down, picked up the lantern, and examined the cover again. This time she checked the stone around the sockets, then the rails behind it. The light stayed well outside the frame.

"If the sheet comes off, those rails still hold the cradle," she said. "If a rail goes with it, we leave the catch in."

"Could it come down anyway?"

"Yes."

I looked at the wedges beneath the weight. One was close to the socket we might break. Above it, the cradle held a mass of stone I could not have shifted with both arms.

Nessa wasn't telling me the plan was safe. She was showing me what would still be holding the weight if it worked.

I checked the way back to the steps before answering.

Nessa stood and pointed toward the platform's empty back wall. It was behind the hatch, several feet to the left of the bent cover.

"Can you make it go that way?"

From beside the steps, I could put the wedge against the fold's right-hand edge and push sideways along the platform toward that wall. The two bolts near the bottom would have to tear loose for the cover to move.

I had seen stone do that. I would have liked to have seen iron do it somewhere more forgiving first.

"I can push that way," I said. "The rest is what worries me."

"It worries me too."

She took the lantern up to its hook in the control room, then returned for the loose bolts and chisel. I waited while she cleared them from the space where the cover might land.

"Only when I am up there," she said.

"Yes."

"And if you change where you want to push, tell me."

I nodded.

*This is a much better instruction than gently.*

She went up to the window. I remained beside the lowest stair, with the moving weight secured behind the guard on my right. The blank wall was to my left. I put the bar's wedge against the side of the fold, clear of the cradle and its chain.

My calf tightened when I bent my knees. I stopped and set my remaining shoe flat on the platform. The leather pad under my other foot shifted. I adjusted it before returning the wedge to the iron.

"Ready?" I asked.

"I am clear."

I kept my grip firm, with the wedge resting against the iron. It hadn't moved. Through the outlet shaft came the rush of water, and above my right shoulder the chain creaked under its load.

Every part of me wanted to brace and shove. I had just tried that. The dent was still beneath the wedge.

I checked the empty wall, then the window above the steps. Nessa was clear of the platform, watching.

*There is time. She is clear. The weight is stopped.*

I let my shoulders loosen. Without changing the wedge's direction, I gave the bar a light push toward the wall.

The cover tore sideways with a sound like a pan being folded in half.

A bolt head struck the wall before I saw it leave its socket. Pale stone burst around the other bolt. The sheet followed, hit the wall, and bounced onto the hatch with a second clang.

I pulled the bar clear and tried to step back onto the stair.

The pad turned beneath my left heel.

For a moment, my foot was on the edge of the leather instead of the floor. I tried to bring my other leg beneath me, but my right knee hit stone before I could catch myself. Pain flashed up the leg. I bent over it, unable to finish the breath I had taken.

Nessa called my name from above.

"Here." I swallowed and tried again, louder. "I am here."

I remained on my knee, keeping my left hand against my thigh. Loose grit rattled across the platform. The weight had moved against the catch when the cover broke, but it had stopped again. I could still see both rails standing behind the guard.

Once the last little piece of stone fell, Nessa brought the lantern down and set it beside me on the lowest stair.

"Stay where you are."

"I had intended to."

She crouched to examine my leg, where the calf bandage was still dry and my knee was developing an opinion of its own.

"Can you move your foot?"

I moved it once, watching my toes instead of her face. They obeyed. When I tried straightening the leg, pain tightened around the knee and I stopped before it began to shake.

"That hurts."

"You landed on stone."

She waited while I bent the leg back to where it had been. I would have preferred to stand up immediately and make the fall less interesting. My knee refused to help.

It was comforting to have the cause confirmed by someone else.

The blue writing appeared when I looked for it.

> **Hit Points:** 6 / 7.
> **Condition:** Bruised, Sore.

One point gone to a shoe made out of someone else's knee protection. The curse had broken the iron. It had done nothing useful about my feet.

I set the bar beside the steps. Nessa put her hand beneath my elbow and waited for me to move my good foot.

I hesitated, looking at where her fingers supported my arm.

"Move your foot. I have your arm."

I shifted toward the lowest stair, letting her guide my elbow without pushing against her. Nothing cracked or flew across the platform. I sat down with my right leg out and her hand still beneath my arm.

I knew ordinary contact was safe. It had been easier to know that while nobody was close enough to test it.

She untied the cord around the leather pad, turned it back beneath my foot, and threaded the heel loop through a different buckle.

"You need this across the back," she said. "Otherwise it folds when you step away."

I watched her tighten the knot and press the strap flat before checking where the pad sat.

"Better?"

I slid my foot along the floor. The pad moved with it instead of turning under the heel.

"Much."

"Tell me if it starts slipping again."

I looked at the new knot, then at her hands. She had fixed it without making me explain how I had managed to fall off a shoe. I tested it once more before drawing my foot back beside the step.

She stood, then gave the weight considerably more attention than she had given my knee. She checked the catch and pin, the chain, and both rails. At the socket where the stone had come away, she ran the end of the chisel along a new shallow crack.

I watched the chisel reach the end of the crack. Nessa moved the lantern to check behind the socket, then followed the rail upward with its light.

I wanted her to speak. I also wanted more time before finding out what else I had broken.

"The cover is off," she said. "The guide is still straight."

"Enough to use?"

"Enough to try. From upstairs."

I drew my foot beneath me. Upstairs sounded very good.

I looked at the twisted sheet on the hatch. I had pictured it coming away like a loose board. It had arrived at the wall as something I would have difficulty recognizing if I had not watched it happen.

"That is more damage than I wanted."

"Yes."

She did not hurry to make the answer kinder.

"The outlet could not open with it there," she added. "Now it might."

My knee took my weight when I stood to collect the bar, though it hurt. Nessa followed me up the five steps and hung the lantern in the control room, bringing the handle's pin only after checking that the catch remained engaged.

The handle reached nearly to the room's threshold. Beside it, a small geared wheel connected to the mechanism below. From there she could work both without standing beside the cradle.

"Watch the gate shaft," she said. "If the chain stops while the weight keeps moving, tell me."

"What should it do?"

"Move together."

I found the two lengths of chain again, one descending to the weight and the other disappearing toward the outlet gate. Nessa withdrew the loose wedges with a long hooked rod from the cupboard. Then she turned the little geared wheel until the weight rose a fraction and the catch no longer carried the load. She raised the catch clear and let the wheel turn back beneath her hands.

The weight descended.

I watched it approach the bright scrape where it had stopped every time before. My fingers closed against my thigh.

It passed the mark.

The chain rolled over the pulley, and a wooden groan rose from the outlet shaft. I looked between the two lengths of chain, trying to keep both in sight. Then water struck something below with a roar that filled the room.

I flinched toward Nessa. Her hands stayed on the handle.

"Moving?"

The chain on the gate's side was still traveling. So was the one above the weight.

"Both chains. Yes."

Near the bottom of the guide, the cradle slowed. Nessa lowered the handle, and I heard the catch meet another tooth through the rush of water. Both chains stopped.

This time, the gate stayed open.

For several breaths, we looked at the reservoir without speaking.

The leaves gathered beneath the nearest lamp began to travel toward the outlet. The little circle they had been making lengthened, pulled apart, and disappeared down the side channel. A pale strip emerged beneath the old water stain on the wall.

Nessa put the pin through the handle's new position.

"It is draining," she said.

I kept my eyes on the pale strip beneath the stain. A little more stone appeared. Then more, wet and dark below the old line.

"I can see it."

My voice was louder than it needed to be. Nessa glanced at me, then turned back to the water. I could have watched that strip widen for the rest of the day.

With a stopped weight, a cleared platform, and someone who knew which part could be broken, I had managed to do something useful. My knee hurt, and the iron cover would not be going back into place.

The water was going down.

For once, I could tell which part of the result I wanted to keep.

---

We waited in the control room while the water fell. Nessa kept checking the outlet chain. I sat on the stool with my right leg straight and watched the dark stain on the wall widen above the surface.

Below us, the tops of the inspection steps emerged, followed by the pale ledge beneath a sheet of retreating water.

It followed the end wall below us toward the opposite bank. Pillars stood along its outer edge, their bases joined by the narrow strip of stone. Near the far bank, the ledge turned toward a small stair below the door with the white rag on its handle.

Nessa pointed across the water.

"Those steps come up beneath that door. From there, the gallery takes us to the service stair above the break."

I traced the route with my eyes, down from our platform and along the end wall, then around the corner to the stairs on the other side.

For the first time, I could see enough of a possible exit to understand how my feet might reach it.

As the surface dropped, water streamed off the ledge through little channels between the pillar bases. Halfway along, sediment had collected against a raised seam, darkening the stone.

"That looks slippery."

"It will be. We wait until we can see where the water is moving."

She took the coil of rope from beside the cupboard and checked it a section at a time. I watched her bend one fuzzy section over her thumb, check the fibers beneath it, and return the coil to the floor.

"Could we go back to the workroom instead?" I asked.

"We could. The water is still entering through the smaller channels. This gives us time while the outlet stays open."

I looked toward the weight.

"And if it closes?"

"We want to be up the other steps before the ledge floods again."

I looked from the weight to the stair on the far bank. We would have to go down toward the water before we could climb away from it. The stool was dry, and my knee had finally stopped aching with every movement.

I brought my foot beneath me anyway. I wanted the door on the other side more than another few minutes sitting here.

While she checked the chain and pin once more, I stood to try my feet. My knee was stiff from sitting, but still bent, and the leather pad stayed beneath my left foot when I stepped backward. Nessa noticed the test as she lifted the bag.

"Still holding?"

"Yes."

"Then put the bar on the bag. You will want a hand free."

She made a loop with a short length of spare cord and secured the bar along the bag's outside, wedge down. I held the bag still while she worked. Carrying something had never seemed especially useful before this place. Now it gave me a job I could do without checking the far wall first.

With the bag over her shoulder and the lantern in hand, she led me down to the platform.

The torn cover still rested across the hatch. Nessa got her wrench beneath its loose corner and tipped it against the empty back wall. The iron scraped over stone, then settled with a heavy clang. I remained on the steps until she had moved clear.

The hatch opened with a rusty squeal, letting cool air reach us from below.

The stair descended along the end wall, with the reservoir beyond its left edge. Water dripped from the lowest treads. A heavy iron ring was fixed beside the opening. Nessa tied the rope to it, then lowered the rest of the coil onto a dry step.

"The brackets along the ledge take a line," she said. "We use one when we inspect the lower gates. I will put it through as we go."

I looked at the ring, then at the drop beyond the stairs.

"Do you do this often?"

"Usually with less water moving."

She tightened the knot, tested it, and went down ahead of me. I stayed at the hatch until she had reached the first dry tread, then followed several steps behind.

I set my remaining shoe flat on each step before bringing the leather pad down beside it. The repaired heel loop held. I checked it anyway.

With each step, the water grew louder. Beneath the outlet shaft, foam and leaves pulled away from the wall in a current strong enough to carry them out of sight before I reached the next tread.

Nessa stopped at the bottom and held the lantern low.

The first stretch of ledge was clear and wide enough to walk facing forward, provided I did not decide to let anyone pass. Keeping to the wall on my right would put the reservoir on my left. Water still crossed the stone near the next pillar, but its edge was visible beneath it.

"Wait there."

She walked to the pillar and secured the first stretch of rope to an iron bracket at her waist. Then she gathered the spare length and turned back.

"Now. Keep to the wall. Use the line for balance, but watch your feet."

The rope was rough and wet inside my left glove. I closed my hand firmly around it before stepping off the last tread.

My good foot found the stone. I brought the other beside it and looked down to check that the pad was flat. The water kept moving at the edge of my sight.

One hand at a time, I moved along the line without pulling it. If my foot slipped, I would want to catch myself with a quick little tug. I looked at the bracket Nessa had just tested and kept my grip steady.

It needed to stay in the wall for both of us.

At the pillar, I stopped beside her. The outlet's rush made conversation difficult. She pointed to a strip of cleaner stone along the wall and waited while I placed my foot on it.

We went on that way, one short stretch at a time. She secured the line at each bracket. I followed once she had checked the next piece of ledge.

The world had become a wall, a line, and the next place to put a foot. I could manage that much.

At the darker patch I had seen from above, Nessa used the flat end of the wrench to scrape sediment away from the raised seam. Water carried the loosened dirt over the edge. She checked both sides with the lantern, then crossed.

I lifted my left foot over the seam instead of dragging the pad across it. Nothing caught. I stood still on the other side for a moment and let myself be pleased.

Then something struck iron inside the wall on my right.

Nessa stopped.

A recessed doorway opened a few paces ahead. Bars filled its frame. Beyond them, a short vestibule led to another barred gate farther inside. Water dripped from the ceiling between the two barriers.

The next impact came from beyond the inner gate, accompanied by the low cry I had heard beneath Malgorth's chamber and across the reservoir. From here, it sounded like something straining to finish a breath it no longer needed.

A shape moved behind the inner bars as Nessa turned her lantern toward the recess.

It had two arms. That was the first thing I checked. They belonged to a body much larger than mine, with a broad belly beneath a torn leather covering. Gray skin hung loose along one shoulder. One side of its mouth had collapsed, leaving teeth visible where the lip should have been.

As it stepped toward the gate, a chain dragged from the shackle around its ankle, stretching back into the dark.

The red writing appeared beside its head.

> **Ogre Zombie**
>
> **Creature:** Ogre Zombie (reanimated ogre).
> **Creature Type:** Undead.
> **Size:** Large.
> **Hit Points:** 85 / 85.
> **Armor Class:** 8 (unarmored).
> **Condition:** Active, Chained.
>
> **Threat:** Lethal within reach.
> **Recommendation:** Keep the gates between you and the creature.

Eighty-five. I had been very pleased to get seven back.

The ogre closed one hand around a bar of the inner gate. Its other arm hung beside its body, and I watched the fingers uncurl.

Then it struck the iron with the heel of its palm.

I felt the impact through the ledge and stopped with my feet together. Above the gate, a pale crack ran through the mortar. I searched its edges for fresh dust, unable to tell how long it had been there.

The ogre drew its arm back again.

I wanted to be past the doorway before it finished the next blow, but Nessa was still in front of me and the water was beside us. There was nowhere to hurry without getting in her way.

"An ogre zombie," I said.

Nessa watched its hand through both sets of bars, the lantern already lowered.

"Keep moving."

"The frame is cracked."

"I see it. We are outside the first gate. It is behind the second. We stay on this side."

She turned the lantern toward the strip of ledge ahead, keeping herself between the light and the bars.

I looked through the first gate to the short space beyond it. If I went in, I would be closer to the ogre and farther from the rope. If I broke anything, I might have to find out what the second gate had been keeping back.

I had put my hand against every serious problem since arriving. Several of them had become different problems immediately afterward.

The bar was tied to the bag. The dagger was wrapped inside my pocket. I could reach neither without stopping in front of something I very much wanted to leave behind.

I followed Nessa.

As we passed the recess, the ogre shifted its face along the bars to keep us in sight. The chain tightened behind it. A long scrape followed, somewhere inside the lower passage.

My hands wanted to close harder around the rope. I kept my grip firm and concentrated on the cleaner strip beside the wall.

One step. Bring the other foot forward. Leave room behind Nessa.

The ogre struck the gate again.

I flinched without turning, keeping my hand closed around the rope. Dust sifted into the vestibule. I watched it settle through the corner of my eye as I brought my padded foot forward.

Neither gate opened.

The pillar ahead began to hide the recess. First the ogre's shoulder disappeared, then its ruined face, leaving only its hand against the bars. I kept moving until I couldn't see that either.

Only then did I stop and look back.

Nessa was waiting at the corner where the end wall met the far bank. She held the lantern toward the next section of ledge.

"You all right?"

"I would like it to remain where it is."

"So would I."

She fed the rope through the corner bracket.

There had been no fight. The writing remained red behind me until the pillar hid it. No new message congratulated me for walking away.

I had two people to get out now. I could count that without a window.

---

We turned left along the far bank. The wall remained on my right, the water on my left. Ahead, the little stair climbed toward the open doorway with the rag. Between us and its first tread, water still crossed the ledge in a narrow sheet.

Nessa stopped before it.

At the foot of the stairs, water poured through a shallow drain whose shifted metal cover left a dark gap along one side. The ledge continued beneath it, with enough stone beside the wall for one foot, but a stronger pulse of water tilted the cover.

"I do not trust that," I said, stopping before the water reached my feet.

"Neither do I."

She set the lantern on a bracket above the drain and took the bar from the bag. I held the bag still while she freed it, looking back toward the corner we had just passed.

The wedge slid beneath the cover's raised corner. Nessa pressed down, shifted her feet, and guided the metal toward its recess. Another pulse of water ran over it. She kept the pressure on until the edge settled with a scrape.

When she lifted the bar clear, I waited for the cover to tilt again.

It stayed in place. She tested it with her boot before lifting the lantern, and I let go of the bag.

My contribution was being available if something needed to become several feet less attached to the floor. This particular repair seemed better suited to someone who could push until it fit.

She put the bar back into its loop on the bag.

"Step where I step."

She crossed the drain close to the wall and reached the first stair. I followed, setting my good foot where hers had been. Cold water reached the cloth around my left toes before I lifted them clear.

The ring's warmth remained faint in my hand. It did very little about a wet sock.

From the first dry step, I checked the makeshift sole, relieved to find the heel cord holding and the pad still beneath my foot.

*You may be ugly. I have become extremely fond of your continued presence.*

I kept walking before fondness could persuade me to pat it.

The stair climbed along the wall beneath the far gallery. Halfway up, it turned sharply right, taking us away from the water. I paused at the turn while Nessa looked through the open doorway above.

The bucket still stood outside the door, its rag moving against the handle. Beyond it, a blue lamp lit the far end of a straight passage, dry apart from old stains near the floor.

Nessa went in first. She set the bag down just inside the door, then held the lantern back toward me.

"Almost there."

I wanted to take the last two steps together. My knee made me take them one at a time.

Across the threshold, I set both feet on dry stone and stopped. There was room to turn around. There was room to let Nessa pass without leaning over water.

For several breaths, I listened to the outlet below us. The ogre struck something again, but the sound reached us through more than one wall, and I no longer had to watch a gate while choosing where to stand.

Nessa looked back down the stair, then across the reservoir toward the weight we had left secured.

"It is still open."

"Can we leave it?"

"The crew will need to check the guide and replace the cover. The open outlet gives them room to work."

She pulled the door shut. Wood scraped over the threshold. The latch caught. She tried the handle once, then took her bag and continued along the passage.

I followed her past a row of narrow windows. Below them, the reservoir stretched toward the landing where I had first seen her lantern. The service gate was a little shape beneath a blue lamp. I could pick out my damaged railing farther along the near bank.

We had followed the end wall from the near bank and come up on the opposite side. My map would need another large correction. I was beginning to understand why people made maps before becoming lost.

The passage turned right at the end of the reservoir. After the turn, a broad flight climbed toward a closed wooden door. White paint marked the stone beside it.

Nessa stopped at the foot of the flight.

"The service stair," she said. "We are above the broken landing."

The steps continued all the way to the door without a gap, a moving slab behind me, or a sword protruding from anyone within sight.

"That is a very good staircase."

Nessa looked at it, then at me.

"It has had a quiet day."

I climbed slowly. My right knee hurt on the higher steps. I used my good leg first, bringing the other up once I had somewhere firm to stand. Nessa waited whenever she reached the edge of the lantern's light.

Near the top, a draft reached the sweat at my neck. I stopped with one foot on the next step.

Wet wood. Pine.

I had found leaves in the water and cold air in several passages. This air came from beneath a door at the top of a staircase someone had built to use. I lifted my head toward it, listening.

A voice came from the other side.

Nessa set the bag on the landing and knocked with the metal ring. Three hard knocks, then two. I had heard that pattern while staring across the reservoir. Here, it traveled through wood and came back from a space larger than the stair.

"Nessa?"

She closed her eyes.

"Yes."

I climbed the last step as footsteps approached. The bolt scraped, and Nessa moved her bag clear before the door opened toward us.

Daylight reached the landing. I raised my hand against it and waited for my eyes to adjust.

A covered work yard stood beyond the door. Split wood was stacked on rain-dark boards. Farther out, pine branches moved beneath a gray sky.

I looked above the branches, then farther along the roof's edge.

The sky kept going.

A man in a heavy coat stood beside the door with a lamp he no longer needed. He looked at Nessa, then at me. His eyes stopped at my empty scabbard and continued down to the leather pad under my foot.

"Who is that?"

I lowered my hand. My introduction had reached the point where several parts needed deciding before I opened my mouth. Summoned champion. Civilian. The person responsible for some of the repairs they were about to need.

Nessa picked up the bag.

"Haruto. He is with me."

The man stepped aside.

I went through the doorway after her, keeping my hands clear of the frame.

Cold air reached my face beneath the roof. I took a breath and could smell the wet wood without dust catching in my throat.

The trees moved beyond the yard. There was a road beside them, with room to walk away from the building, and I kept looking at it while Nessa spoke to the man.

There would be questions about the lodge, the palace, and the very large problem attached to my hands. For now, I could see where I would put my feet next.

Nessa glanced back.

"Coming?"

"Yes."

I followed her.
